2. Seal an encrypted packet

Each export uses a new random 256-bit AES-GCM key derived from your passphrase with PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 iterations). LightUp never stores, recovers, or transmits your passphrase.

Client-review exports require each selected file to be marked Prepared for human review (NOT approved); unclassified, unfinished and historical drafts are refused. They always strip internal notes and preparation stages, even from client-labelled files. Historical drafts are refused. A bounded local check also refuses some common credential patterns, but CANNOT certify that a document contains no secrets. They also refuse operator-labelled or unclassified files; every encrypted export shows the exact selected filenames for a final local confirmation. This routing choice never authenticates the recipient or grants rights.

To prepare a smaller recipient handoff, select only the intended files in the inventory. All files remain selected by default. Never treat an encrypted handoff as recipient identity or consent.

To rotate a forgotten, exposed or old passphrase after unlocking your existing packet, enter a new password above and choose Re-encrypt entire private archive. Downloading a new copy cannot revoke any previously shared encrypted file.

3. Restore a saved packet

Load one locally saved encrypted vault. Its encrypted inner payload also preserves the self-reported intended handoff route when present. Its authenticity depends on knowing the passphrase and trusting where you got the file. Encryption alone does not prove client identity, consent, scope approval or authorization.
